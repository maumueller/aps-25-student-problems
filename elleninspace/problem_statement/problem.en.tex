\problemname{Ellen in Space}

Officer Ellen finds herself in trouble, alone deep in space.

After an accident on her spaceship, Ellen and her cat had to escape the ship, on their space shuttle.
The shuttle only has limited fuel and communication capability, so Ellen's best plan is to set course for known space, and hope a space station will find her.

Ellen is at the edge of known space, so all the space stations are in front of her space shuttle.
In the space shuttle's navigation computer, the shuttle is in point $(0,0,0)$
Luckily for Ellen, her computer knows the location of the $N$ nearby company space stations.
Locations are given as $(x,y,z)$ coordinates, in parsecs.
All company space stations have a powerful radar, capable of detecting ships within a radius of 1 parsec.

The shuttle only has fuel for a single thrust.
Thereafter, it flies silently through space, in a straight line.

Since company space stations are unreliable, Ellen plans her course, so she will pass within radar distance of as many space stations as possible.
This gives her the best chance of being picked up.

Ellen wants to know her chances, in particular, she wants to know the number of space stations she will pass on her best course.

\section*{Input}
The first line contains the integer $0<N\leq100$, the number of space stations.
Then follows $N$ lines of the coordinates of real numbers $x, y, z$ of each space station, where $1.0\leq x\leq100.0$, and $-100.0\leq y,z \leq 100.0$.

The input will be such that the answer does not change even if the radius of the radar were 
$1+10^{-3}$ long.

\section*{Output}
The highest number of stations Ellen can pass by, when flying in a straight line.
